# Last Call — User Personas

> Who uses Last Call, what they want, and what gets in their way.

---

## Persona 1 — The Value-Seeking Customer

**Name:** Hamza, 24
**Location:** Gulshan-e-Iqbal, Karachi
**Occupation:** Junior software developer
**Monthly income:** Rs. 60,000
**Devices:** Android phone (primary), laptop for work

### About Hamza

Hamza lives alone and eats out 4–5 times a week. He loves good food but watches his budget carefully. He regularly checks Foodpanda for deals but finds delivery fees add up quickly. He pays for almost everything through JazzCash because he doesn't have a credit card.

He is aware of food waste as a concept but has never had a reason to act on it. He'll adopt any app that saves him money without extra effort.

### What He Wants
- Quality restaurant food at prices that make sense on his salary
- Fast, frictionless experience — he wants to decide and pick up in under 5 minutes
- To discover good restaurants he wouldn't normally try at full price
- A reason to brag to friends ("bhai, got biryani for Rs. 120 at this amazing place")

### Pain Points
- Delivery fees make food expensive even when the base price is low
- He can't afford to eat at most restaurants near him at regular prices
- He wastes time searching for deals across multiple platforms

### How He Uses Last Call
- Opens the app on his way home from work (6–9 PM peak)
- Filters by his area (Gulshan)
- Picks the most value-for-money bag from a restaurant he's curious about
- Pays via JazzCash — done in 30 seconds
- Picks up the bag, shows pickup code, goes home satisfied

### What Would Make Him Leave
- App shows bags that are already sold out (stale inventory)
- Pickup experience is awkward or embarrassing at the restaurant
- Payment fails or is unreliable

## Persona 2 — The Sustainability-Conscious Customer

**Name:** Aisha, 29
**Location:** DHA Phase 6, Karachi
**Occupation:** Marketing manager at a multinational
**Monthly income:** Rs. 150,000
**Devices:** iPhone, MacBook

### About Aisha

Aisha earns well and can afford to eat wherever she wants. But she cares deeply about sustainability and environmental issues. She follows international food waste trends and has heard of Too Good To Go from friends who've lived abroad.

She is Last Call's most likely early adopter and loudest brand ambassador. She'll post about it on Instagram, tell her friends, and leave detailed reviews.

### What She Wants
- To feel good about her purchases — the CO₂ saved number matters to her
- Discovery: trying restaurants she hasn't been to before
- A polished, premium-feeling app experience
- Social proof: ratings, reviews, partner quality

### Pain Points
- Most Pakistani apps feel low-quality and unreliable
- No easy way to act on sustainability values in daily life
- Finds food waste by restaurants genuinely upsetting

### How She Uses Last Call
- Browses the featured section for new partners to try
- Checks the CO₂ impact stat before and after purchase
- Leaves reviews to help other customers
- Shares the app with friends in her social circle
- Uses it as a conversation starter ("did you know this restaurant saves food this way?")

### What Would Make Her Leave
- Low-quality food or misleading bag descriptions
- App feels unprofessional or buggy
- Partner restaurants have consistently poor reviews

## Persona 3 — The Partner Restaurant Owner

**Name:** Usman, 42
**Location:** Burns Road, Karachi
**Business:** Usman's Desi Kitchen — 8 years old, 40 seats, Rs. 150,000/month revenue
**Tech comfort:** Moderate — uses WhatsApp daily, struggles with apps

### About Usman

Usman runs a popular biryani restaurant on Burns Road. He's proud of his food but loses Rs. 15,000–20,000 worth of biryani every week to waste. He's heard about Last Call from a neighbour restaurant owner and is curious but cautious.

He doesn't have time to learn new software. He needs something that works the way he already works — through WhatsApp notifications, not app dashboards. He'll trust Last Call if the first week goes smoothly.

### What He Wants
- To recover money from food he'd otherwise throw away
- Minimum change to his existing workflow
- Clear, predictable payouts — no surprises
- Simple verification at pickup (he doesn't trust paper slips)
- To understand exactly how much he's earning and what Last Call is taking

### Pain Points
- Tech-averse staff — they won't learn a complicated system
- Worried about rush at closing time if many customers arrive at once
- Unsure if Last Call customers are the "right" demographic for his restaurant
- Confused by commission structures from other platforms

### How He Uses Last Call
- At 7 PM, he (or his cashier) opens the partner app and creates a bag: "Biryani Mix Box, Rs. 200, 5 available, pickup 8:30–9:30 PM"
- Gets WhatsApp notification when an order comes in
- Staff use the app to mark orders as Ready when packing begins
- At pickup, cashier enters customer's 6-digit code to verify identity
- Confirms cash received, marks order as complete
- Every Monday morning, receives a WhatsApp saying "Rs. X has been sent to your JazzCash"

### What Would Make Him Leave
- Customers who don't show up (no-show problem)
- Payout delays or confusion
- Complex onboarding that takes more than 10 minutes

## Persona 4 — The Partner Bakery Owner

**Name:** Sana, 35
**Location:** Clifton, Karachi
**Business:** Sana's Patisserie — 4 years old, specialises in cakes and pastries
**Tech comfort:** High — active on Instagram, uses Canva for marketing

### About Sana

Sana's bakery produces fresh products daily. Anything not sold by 8 PM has to be discarded because it won't be fresh the next morning. She wastes Rs. 8,000–12,000 of product weekly. She's been following Too Good To Go on Instagram and thinks Last Call is exactly what she needs.

She is tech-savvy, has strong branding instincts, and will want her bakery to look good on the platform (photo quality, description).

### What She Wants
- A platform that matches her brand quality
- Ability to add good photos of her bags to attract customers
- Flexible bag creation (different types on different days)
- Insight into which bags sell best (so she can plan better)
- Digital payment options — her customers prefer JazzCash and SadaPay

### Pain Points
- Daily waste feels like a personal failure
- Instagram followers ask where to find discounted treats but she has no answer
- Existing delivery platforms charge too much for the unit economics to work

### How She Uses Last Call
- Creates different bag types per day (e.g. "Pastry Box" vs "Cake Slice Pack")
- Uploads a beautiful photo of the bag contents
- Monitors the analytics tab to see which bags sell most
- Uses SadaPay as her preferred payout method
- Responds to reviews to build her reputation on the platform

## Persona 5 — The Operations Admin

**Name:** Bilal, 27
**Location:** Karachi (Last Call team)
**Role:** Operations & Partner Success

### About Bilal

Bilal is a Last Call team member responsible for approving new partners, handling disputes, monitoring order health, and ensuring weekly payouts go out correctly. He is not an engineer but is comfortable with web apps.

### What He Needs
- A clear dashboard showing today's key numbers at a glance
- Quick ability to approve or suspend partner applications
- Ability to see and resolve stuck or disputed orders
- One-click payout triggering with status tracking
- No technical knowledge required to do his job

### How He Uses Last Call Admin
- Starts each morning by checking the Dashboard (KPIs, pending approvals)
- Reviews new partner applications — checks CNIC, business license, then approves
- Monitors orders tab for anything anomalous (too many cancellations, disputes)
- Monday mornings: reviews the payout run summary, marks settlements as complete
- Updates promo banners for campaigns (Ramadan, Eid, etc.)

### What Would Make His Job Harder
- Admin panel is slow or crashes
- No way to see which partners are underperforming
- Payout process requires spreadsheet work outside the system
