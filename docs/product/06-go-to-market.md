# Last Call — Go-To-Market Strategy

> How Last Call enters the market, grows its partner supply, builds customer demand, and scales across Pakistan.

---

## The Two-Sided Marketplace Problem

Last Call faces the classic marketplace chicken-and-egg problem:

- **Customers won't download the app** if there aren't enough partner restaurants listed
- **Partners won't list bags** if no customers are buying them

**Our solution:** Launch hyper-locally, with a supply-first approach. We sign up enough partners in one neighbourhood before we start marketing to customers in that neighbourhood. This ensures every customer who opens the app sees real, available bags — not an empty screen.

---

## Phase 1 — Karachi Launch (Months 1–3)

### Target Launch Neighbourhoods (in order)

We launch neighbourhood by neighbourhood, not city-wide. Starting zones:

1. **Burns Road** — Pakistan's most famous food street. High density of restaurants, bakeries, and dessert spots. Strong foot traffic. Media-friendly.
2. **DHA** — Affluent customers, tech-savvy, high smartphone penetration. Many branded restaurants and cafés with consistent surplus.
3. **Clifton** — Similar to DHA. Strong café and bakery culture.
4. **Gulshan-e-Iqbal** — Largest residential area in Karachi. Middle-income target customers. High volume potential.

**Success threshold per neighbourhood before expanding:**
Minimum 15 active partners listing bags on a given weekday before we activate marketing for that area.

---

### Partner Acquisition Strategy (Supply Side)

**Week 1–4: Direct Sales on the Ground**

The single most effective way to sign up restaurants is face-to-face. We hire 3–5 field sales representatives who visit restaurants during slow hours (2–5 PM) with a simple pitch:

*"You're going to throw that food away tonight. We'll help you sell it instead. You keep 80%. It takes 2 minutes to set up."*

**Key objections and how we handle them:**

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "I don't have time for another app" | "Creating a bag takes 90 seconds. You only do it once per day." |
| "Will these customers be the right type?" | "They're paying customers who chose your restaurant. Same as any customer." |
| "What if no one buys?" | "No sale, no commission. Zero risk to you." |
| "I don't trust online payments" | "You can offer cash pickup — no digital payments needed to start." |
| "How do I know you'll pay me?" | "Weekly, every Monday, to your JazzCash. We show you the calculation in the app." |

**Target restaurant types for early acquisition:**
- Biryani and karahi restaurants (high daily surplus)
- Bakeries (100% daily waste if unsold)
- Fast food and snacks restaurants near offices
- Cafés and dessert spots

---

### Customer Acquisition Strategy (Demand Side)

**Primary channel: Word of mouth + social proof**

The first 1,000 customers will come from:

1. **Instagram & TikTok content** — Videos of bags being revealed (the "unboxing" moment). Pakistani food content performs extremely well on social. Partner with food micro-influencers in Karachi (5K–50K followers).

2. **Office building partnerships** — Partner with 3–5 large offices near our launch areas. Pitch the app to HR teams as an employee benefit. One company activation can generate 50–200 app downloads instantly.

3. **University campuses** — Student ambassadors at IBA, LUMS (Karachi), and NED University. Students are price-sensitive and highly social. One student who loves the app tells 20 friends.

4. **WhatsApp group campaigns** — Residents' WhatsApp groups in DHA and Clifton are highly active. A well-worded "did you know?" message seeded in 10 groups can go viral locally.

5. **Referral programme activation** — Our in-app referral system (free discount for both parties) turns every customer into a recruiter. We prime this with a launch period: first 500 referrals give Rs. 50 credit to both parties instead of the standard Rs. 30.

---

### PR & Media Strategy

**Earned media angles:**

1. **Food waste story** — "Pakistan wastes Rs. 1 trillion of food annually. These founders are trying to fix it." Suitable for Dawn, The News, Geo, ARY.

2. **Startup story** — Pakistani tech startup story. Suitable for Startup Pakistan, ProPakistani, TechJuice.

3. **Restaurant owner story** — "This Burns Road restaurant now earns Rs. 40,000/month from food it used to throw away." Human interest angle.

4. **Environmental angle** — Social media post showing cumulative CO₂ prevented by Last Call users. Environmental content resonates with young urban Pakistanis.

**Target publications/channels:**
- Digital: Dawn.com, ProPakistani, TechJuice, Startup.pk
- Print: Dawn Instep, The News Business section
- TV: Geo News startup segments, ARY Digital lifestyle slots
- YouTube/TikTok: Food vloggers in Karachi

## Phase 2 — Karachi Scale (Months 4–6)

Once Burns Road, DHA, and Clifton are running smoothly, expand to:
- Saddar, Gulshan, North Nazimabad, PECHS, Malir, Bahria Town
- Target: 200 active partners citywide, 10,000 monthly active customers

**New tactics at this stage:**
- In-app promotion banners for new areas ("Gulshan is now live! 12 partners near you")
- Partner referral programme: existing partners refer new restaurant owners and receive a bonus
- Loyalty programme for customers: after 10 orders, get a free bag

---

## Phase 3 — Lahore Launch (Month 7–9)

Lahore is Pakistan's food capital — arguably the strongest market for Last Call after Karachi.

**Launch strategy:** Repeat the playbook. Hire a Lahore-based field team. Focus first on Gulberg and DHA Lahore. Partner with Lahore food bloggers and influencers.

**Key difference from Karachi:** Lahore's food culture is more celebratory — larger portions, more expensive restaurants. Average bag price may be higher (Rs. 200–300). Adjust pricing guidance accordingly.

---

## Phase 4 — Islamabad Launch (Month 10–12)

Islamabad's market is smaller but high-value. Higher income consumers, more cafés and international franchises, more sustainability awareness among the educated urban population.

**Key targets:** F-7, F-10, F-11 (high-income areas), Blue Area corporate customers, university campuses (NUST, FAST, Quaid-i-Azam).

---

## Competitive Strategy

### Why we win against copycats

If Last Call succeeds, others will try to copy it. Our moat:

1. **First-mover network effects** — The restaurant that joined Last Call first already has loyal customers. A new entrant has to offer more to lure that restaurant away.

2. **Partner habits** — Once a restaurant manager builds the habit of listing on Last Call every evening, switching has friction. Especially if their staff knows the workflow.

3. **Customer discovery** — Customers who have 5 favourite restaurants on Last Call won't switch to a copycat that doesn't have those same restaurants.

4. **Brand association** — Being the "food rescue" brand in Pakistan has value. Too Good To Go has brand equity in Europe that no copycat has displaced.

5. **Payout reliability** — The restaurant that received 6 months of accurate Monday payouts trusts Last Call. Trust is not transferable.
